Nod the Beat guide

A white line-art character on a light frosted scrim, 3.5 seconds: tilt left, tilt right, then a nod with a shout and a hair flip. The label under it follows each move (Tilt left, Tilt right, Nod & say hey!, Let’s go!) and the round starts when it ends. The same line style guides all four games.

Drawn in code

Club lightsA kick-synced pulse on the background, two sweeping beams in the accent colours, and a white flash on every hit.
Frosted bandA blurred, semi-transparent strip behind the crowd so they read against the camera feed without a full background.
Platter ringsJudge rings on the two platters with an approach disc per note and a pulse ring after each hit, the same system as Wink Wink.
ResultConfetti on a good round, a big percentage, a verdict line, the crowd’s final reaction, and the face stickers above.

Sound

All synthesized with WebAudio except the ending stinger.

GrooveThree live grooves, one dealt at random per round and never the same twice in a row: Neon Funk at 80 bpm (square bass, 16th hats, snare), Deep House at 88 bpm (four-on-the-floor, open hats, claps, seventh-chord stabs) and Lo-fi Bounce at 74 bpm (half-time kick, swung hats, long chords). All synthesized with WebAudio and scheduled ahead so the chart stays locked to the beat.
Hit soundsEach hit plays the next note of a 12-note riff (C5 up to C6 and back), so a clean run sounds like a melody. A drop adds a chord and a synthesized “yo!”. Misses scratch the record and draw a low boo.
CrowdCheer on a good hit, groan on a miss. On a good round the five club-goers hop in turn, staggered down the line.
ShoutNod the Beat: a synthesized “hey” or “yo” (a falling sawtooth through two formant filters) when a shout note lands.
StingerA good round (50% or better) ends with a chord hit, a crowd roar with claps on the beat, and ending-great.mp3, shared with the salon and jump-rope games. Below 50%, the record scratch and a boo.

Rules & input

Two inputs, one chart.

Round15 seconds. Notes fall onto the left and right platters; hit them on the beat. A drop wants both platters at once. Nod the Beat adds two shout notes per round.
Blink DJLeft eye for the left platter, right eye for the right, both eyes for the drop. Same eye tracking as Wink Wink: blendshape difference over 0.18, 70 ms single-vs-both window, 40 ms lag compensation. Perfect ±280 ms, Good ±600 ms.
Nod the BeatTilt left or right for the platters, nod for the drop. Roll is the angle of the eye line; pitch is the nose drop over the eye-to-chin height, so turning the head does not read as a nod, and nods are ignored while the head is turned. A tilt of about 5° triggers, and a quick flick counts early. Coming back 3.4° from the peak re-arms the same side. When a tilt and a nod read at once, the stronger one wins. Perfect ±220 ms, Good ±500 ms.
ShoutNod the Beat: one shout note every eight beats, alternating HEY! and YO!. It counts on a jump in loudness, not speech: the threshold rides at 3× the room’s floor and 2.4× the last half second, so the groove from the speaker does not trigger it. Window ±650 ms. No shout notes if the mic is refused.
ChartWink DJ: left–right with a drop every eighth beat. Nod the Beat changes every round: the opening side is random, the drop sits on beat 6 or 7 of each eight, about one beat in eight rests, and up to two notes can land on the same side in a row. First note lands about 2.7 s after GO.
Safe zones390 × 694 canvas. Platters inside the core zone (x 65–325, y 82–533); the crowd below the booth stays inside the visual zone. Nod the Beat has no score pills over the face. ?zones shows the overlay, ?debug logs detection.
FlowCamera card (Nod the Beat asks for camera and mic) → guide on a frosted scrim (“Blink to start” for Wink DJ, the 3.5-second line-art warm-up for Nod the Beat) → 15 s → result with face stickers.
